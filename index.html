Overview

There is no trail around Callaghan Lake, so reaching Cirque Lake takes a boat. You paddle about 2 km across to the far shore, find the creek and climb a short, steep, unofficial route to a granite bowl holding a clear alpine lake.

BC Parks says Callaghan Lake Park has no formally developed or maintained hiking trails. Whistler.com rates the Cirque Lake hike moderate to difficult, and other guides call it intermediate because the route is rugged and poorly marked.

Getting there

The drive

Turn off Highway 99 (70 km north of Vancouver, 20 km south of Whistler) onto the paved Callaghan Valley Road for 8 km, then onto Callaghan Forest Service Road 01 for 8 km of gravel to the lake.

The road

BC Parks says the gravel road is only suitable for high-clearance vehicles because of deep water-bars and cross-ditches. It is closed by snow, usually from November to early June, and one guide reports the gate is closed until mid-May.

The launch

The campground and launch are at the lake. BC Parks lists a pit toilet at the campground (not maintained in winter), no garbage service and minimal cell coverage. Pack out everything.

The route, step by step

  1. 1

    Launch and paddle across

    ≈ 2 km each way≈ 45 minCold glacial water
    • Paddle about 2 km toward the sound of the waterfall and the stream of runoff on the far shore.
    • Launch early. Afternoon wind in the valley can make the return crossing hard work.
    • Wear a PFD or stay close to shore: the water is very cold.
  2. 2

    Land and find the trail

    Marshy landingBugs
    • Land on the grassy, marshy patch beside the stream and expect lots of bugs.
    • The trail is not well marked, but a worn track is usually visible. Follow the river upstream and cross it, on logs in summer.
    • Orange markers or tape may be present, depending on what survived winter.
  3. 3

    Climb to the lake

    ≈ 1.6–2 km one way≈ 360 m gainSteep, wet, rooty
    • The first section is short, steep and often wet and slippery. Ropes are present and can help.
    • The trail then opens onto a rocky slope: stay left or pick your own line.
    • Turn around on the climb for views back over Callaghan Lake.
  4. 4

    Arrive at the bowl

    Highest point ≈ 1,544 m
    • Plan your day around the return paddle, not the departure.
    • Whistler.com and other guides suggest allowing a full day.

Know before you go

Grizzlies and black bears

BC Parks says grizzly bears live in the park and black bears are present. Make noise, keep food and scented items secured, and see BC Parks' wildlife safety advice.

Dogs

BC Parks: dogs are not allowed in the Cirque Lake Basin, which is grizzly bear habitat. Elsewhere in the park they must be leashed and only at the campground, day-use area or in a boat.

Wind

Afternoon wind can turn the return crossing into hard work. Launch early.

Cold water

Glacial water is very cold. Wear a PFD for the whole crossing.

Unofficial route

Navigation is largely on your own. Carry a map and a survival kit, and leave a trip plan with someone.

Remote

BC Parks says cell coverage is minimal and suggests a satellite phone for emergencies.

A remote trip dressed up as a short one

The distances look modest, but you are combining open alpine water, an unmarked route, grizzly country and a valley with no reliable cell coverage. Tell someone your plan, launch early and wear the PFDs.

When to go

Most guides put the season at July to September. BC Parks says the access road is closed when snow covers it, usually from November to early June.

Questions

Can you hike to Cirque Lake without a boat?

No. There is no trail around Callaghan Lake, so the practical way is to paddle roughly 2 km to the far shore. Paddle boards and small inflatables are also used.

How hard is the climb?

Whistler.com rates it moderate to difficult. Figures vary: about 3.2 km round trip with 361 m of gain in one guide, and about 2 km one way and 300 m of gain in others. The first section is steep and slippery.

What is the road like?

BC Parks says the gravel Callaghan Forest Service Road is only suitable for high-clearance vehicles, with deep water-bars and cross-ditches.

Can I bring my dog?

Not into the Cirque Lake Basin, which BC Parks describes as grizzly bear habitat. In the park, dogs must be leashed and are allowed only at the campground, day-use area or in a boat.

Can I camp?

BC Parks allows camping only in the designated campground at Callaghan Lake. Lakeshore and other backcountry camping is not allowed. Check BC Parks for reservations and fees.

Are there bears?

Yes. BC Parks says grizzly bears live in the park and black bears are also present. Make noise and keep food and scented items secured.
